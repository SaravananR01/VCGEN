1 · Demand Coverage
What this measures: For each skill students voted for, we compare student-hours allocated to topics teaching that skill (weighted by confidence) against the fair share implied by the vote fraction. Coverage = min(allocated / fair_share, 1) per skill, averaged across all demanded skills. A score of 100% means every demanded skill received at least its proportional share.

2 · Skill Alignment (Spearman ρ)
What this measures: Spearman's rank correlation between (a) how often students voted for each skill and (b) how many student-hours went to topics covering it. ρ = +1 → schedule perfectly mirrors student priorities. ρ = 0 → no relationship. A p-value ≤ 0.05 confirms statistical significance.

5 · Hour Distribution (Gini)
What this measures: Gini coefficient of student-hour allocation across topics. A moderate Gini (0.3–0.5) is expected and healthy — it means the LP correctly concentrated hours on high-demand topics. Too low → uniform (demand ignored). Too high → monopolised.
